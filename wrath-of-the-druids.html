The challenge

Creating a new land without breaking the franchise.

The core challenge was to give Ireland a strong and memorable visual identity inside Assassin’s Creed Valhalla. The expansion had to feel fully connected to the larger game, yet distinctive enough that players would immediately sense they had entered a different territory, with its own atmosphere, culture, tensions and narrative tone.

This meant finding the right balance between continuity and differentiation: preserving the production reality, the established franchise language and the existing quality bar, while defining new environmental signatures, new tonal references and a clearer sense of place across landscapes, settlements, props, materials, characters and visual mood.

My responsibility

Vision, alignment and follow-through.

My role was to define and maintain the visual direction of this new territory through production.

  • Establish the artistic intent for Ireland as a territory inside Valhalla.
  • Guide atmosphere, environment direction and overall mood.
  • Align multiple art disciplines around a coherent target quality.
  • Support concept, textures, props, VFX, characters and environment teams with clear direction and feedback.
  • Ensure that creative ambition remained compatible with production constraints and delivery realities.
This case study is intentionally written as a direction case study, not as a claim of sole authorship over all final images. The goal is to show decision-making, visual leadership and production alignment.

Visual thesis

Three pillars for the territory.

The territory needed a readable artistic thesis. The following three pillars structure the page and later help explain why the final direction feels coherent.

Mythic nature

Ireland needed a strong feeling of natural presence: rich, alive, sometimes spiritual, and able to support mystery without abandoning grounded credibility.

Territorial identity

The expansion required a sense of difference in vegetation, settlement language, atmosphere and tone so that Ireland would not feel like a duplicate of England or Norway.

Production clarity

The direction had to be clear enough for teams to execute consistently, with decisions that could scale across assets, locations and disciplines.

Key decisions

From taste to production choices.

Recruiters and creative leads need to understand not only what looked good, but what was decided, why it was decided, and how those decisions changed the work.

Decision 01 — Differentiate the atmosphere

Ireland needed a more singular atmospheric identity: a mood that could support mystery, vegetation density and a slightly more lyrical environmental character while remaining readable and game-ready.

Decision 02 — Reinforce territorial signatures

Environmental cues, material balance, settlement rhythms and compositional motifs needed to communicate “Ireland” quickly, so the expansion would not be perceived as a reskin.

Decision 03 — Keep the direction scalable

The visual target had to remain understandable for multiple teams and disciplines, making the art direction actionable in a production context rather than purely conceptual.

Decision 04 — Protect franchise continuity

Every change had to sit inside the wider Valhalla universe. Distinction was necessary, but franchise coherence remained non-negotiable.
